# Product Brief

## Overview

HeatRadar is a mobile-first growth tracking app for independent music artists. It helps artists quickly understand whether their music is gaining traction, why their Heat Score changed, and what listener movement is worth paying attention to.

HeatRadar is not meant to feel like enterprise analytics software. It should feel like the app an artist opens when they want to know: "Is something happening with my music?"

## Target Audience

Primary users:

- independent artists
- emerging musicians
- artist managers helping small rosters
- artists who release music independently and promote through social platforms

User mindset:

- wants feedback without digging through dashboards
- cares about signs of growth
- wants encouragement, not pressure
- needs clarity around what is actually moving
- may have small numbers but still wants to feel progress

## Core Problem

Music performance data is scattered across platforms and often feels cold, fragmented, or hard to interpret. Artists can see streams, saves, views, likes, and followers, but they often cannot tell what it means emotionally or strategically.

HeatRadar solves the translation problem:

- What changed?
- Why did it matter?
- Is this release gaining heat?
- Is the audience responding?
- Should I be excited, patient, or take action?

## Value Proposition

HeatRadar gives artists a simple, rewarding read on audience traction, engagement movement, release performance, and Heat Score growth.

Instead of forcing users to inspect raw analytics, HeatRadar surfaces:

- Heat Score movement
- what contributed to the score
- meaningful notifications
- release traction signals
- engagement spikes
- audience growth moments
- shareable milestones

## Product Philosophy

HeatRadar should be:

- simple before comprehensive
- emotionally rewarding before analytical
- transparent before magical
- culturally relevant before corporate
- mobile-first before dashboard-first

The product should avoid:

- productivity/task management framing
- admin tools
- generic creator SaaS language
- dense charting
- unexplained scoring
- anxiety-inducing alerts

## Emotional UX Direction

The emotional promise is:

"Something exciting might be happening with my music."

The app should make artists feel:

- encouraged
- validated
- curious
- motivated
- proud of small wins

Empty states should still feel hopeful. Notifications should feel earned. Heat Score should feel understandable. Premium features should feel like deeper clarity, not withheld basic value.

## Monetization Strategy

Current prototype monetization:

- 7-day free trial
- `$4.99/month` subscription screen
- locked premium feature states
- upgrade CTAs

Likely premium value:

- full Heat Score breakdown
- cross-platform traction reads
- weekly HeatRadar recap
- shareable milestone cards
- advanced notification controls
- deeper release and audience history

Monetization should not block the initial emotional reward. Artists should understand why HeatRadar is useful before seeing hard paywall pressure.

## MVP Scope

MVP should include:

- onboarding
- Heat Score dashboard
- Heat Score education
- notifications center
- release performance
- audience movement
- platform connections
- settings/profile
- trial/paywall preview
- empty states
- loading/sync states

MVP should not include:

- label tools
- productivity/task systems
- collaboration workflows
- advanced benchmarking
- AI strategy generation
- complex analytics dashboards

## Future Vision

HeatRadar can become the artist's daily pulse for music movement:

- real-time traction signals
- meaningful notification moments
- shareable wins
- platform-wide growth picture
- simple release intelligence
- eventually predictive insights

The future product should still protect the original feeling: simple, premium, artist-centered, emotionally rewarding.
